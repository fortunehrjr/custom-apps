How to use it

Copy like you always do

Install and open it once

Ferry starts by itself at login after that. Look for ⇅ in the menu bar.

Copy files in Finder

Drag footage to your SSD, or from a card to your Mac. Ferry pops up right away.

Watch it, or don't

Shrink it with the chevron, hide it with ✕. Ferry remembers your choice.

Done

It shows "Copy complete" with the average speed, then slips away. Past copies are in History.

Install

Install in a minute

The same steps work for every app, including the one-time "Open Anyway" step.

Open the installer

Download the app's .dmg, then double-click it. A window opens with the app and an Applications shortcut.

Drag it to Applications

Drop the app icon onto Applications. Then eject the installer (the ⏏ next to it in Finder).

Open it

Open Applications and double-click the app. Menu bar apps (Clipper, Ferry, Now Playing, Stash) show an icon at the top right.

Allow it the first time

macOS says it "cannot verify" the app, because it's not from the App Store. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then confirm. Only once.

Uninstall

Quit the app from its menu, then drag it from Applications to the Trash. To stop an app opening at login, also check System Settings › General › Login Items.

New versions

Updating is painless

Got a newer version from a friend, or built one yourself? Here's how to swap it in without losing your settings.

Quit the app

Use Quit in its menu bar icon or app menu, so the old copy isn't running.

Open the new .dmg

Double-click the new installer you received.

Drag to Applications, choose Replace

macOS asks whether to replace the existing app. Click Replace.

Open it again

Your settings stay exactly as they were. If an app asks for a permission again, just allow it.

What's kept when you update

  • All your settings and choices
  • Ferry's transfer history
  • Orbit's timer lengths and today's sessions
  • Stash's clipboard history and pinned clips
  • Clipper's and Stash's Accessibility permission

Ferry may ask again for Accessibility permission if you use its "Ferry" mode.

Under the hood

Built with

All five are native Mac apps written in Swift. No web views, no Electron, no tracking. That's why they're tiny and fast.

Shared foundation

SwiftSwiftUIAppKitmacOS 14 – 26Universal (Apple silicon + Intel)Ad-hoc code signing

Clipper

Uses the Mac's Accessibility feature (the same one screen readers use) to move and size windows, and listens for your keyboard shortcuts. To keep a window on top, it shows a live picture of it above everything else.

Built with swiftc from a shell script, no Xcode project. Windows move through AXUIElement; drag-to-snap watches mouse events; hot keys use Carbon RegisterEventHotKey. Keep on Top mirrors the window into a floating NSPanel with ScreenCaptureKit window capture, streaming only while it's covered. An identifier-based designated requirement keeps the Accessibility grant across rebuilds.

AXUIElementCarbon hot keysNSEvent monitorsScreenCaptureKitSwiftUI onboarding

Ferry

Reads how fast each drive is reading and writing, and listens to the progress Finder already shares, to know file names, sizes and percentages.

IOKit IOBlockStorageDriver statistics give per-drive byte counters. FSEvents spots new files so Ferry can subscribe with Progress.addSubscriber(forFileURL:) to Finder's copy progress, which also allows cancel(). The window is a non-activating NSPanel in NSGlassEffectView.

IOKitFSEventsNSProgressSwift ChartsWidgetKitNSGlassEffectView

Now Playing

Asks Spotify what's playing, the same way the Shortcuts app can, and shows it in your menu bar and as a widget.

AppleScript over Apple Events (NSAppleScript) polls Spotify off the main thread and sends play/pause/skip. A MenuBarExtra hosts the mini player; a WidgetKit extension renders the same SwiftUI layouts.

Apple EventsMenuBarExtraWidgetKitXcodeGen

Orbit

A timer with a hand-drawn dial, plus a music card that can talk to Spotify, Music and YouTube, or play files from your Mac.

A Swift Package with an @Observable timer model. AppleScript bridges to Spotify, Music, Chrome and Safari, AVFoundation for local files, Liquid Glass via glassEffect, alerts via UserNotifications.

Swift Package ManagerObservationAVFoundationLiquid Glass

Stash

Notices each time you copy something, saves it on your Mac, and shows your history in a glass panel when you press a shortcut.

A tolerant timer compares NSPasteboard.changeCount twice a second and reads the clipboard only when it changes, skipping org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType. The panel is a non-activating NSPanel in NSGlassEffectView, opened by a Carbon hot key; "Paste for me" posts ⌘V with CGEvent.
